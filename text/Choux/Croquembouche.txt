Name:
Croquembouche
Information:
Croquembouche is a French dessert that consists of a tower of small cream-filled choux pastries, also known as profiteroles, that are dipped in caramel and stacked into a cone shape. 
The caramel hardens and creates a crunchy outer shell while the inside remains soft and creamy. Croquembouche is often served at weddings, holidays, and other special occasions.
Ingredients:
1/2 cup (1 stick) unsalted butter
1 cup water
1/2 teaspoon salt
1 tablespoon sugar
1 1/4 cups all-purpose flour
4 large eggs
2 cups whole milk
1/2 cup granulated sugar
4 large egg yolks
1/4 cup cornstarch
2 teaspoons vanilla extract
1/4 teaspoon salt
1 cup heavy cream
2 cups granulated sugar
1/2 cup water
1/4 cup corn syrup
Cost per serving ($):
11.30
Recipe:
1. Preheat the oven to 400°F. Line two baking sheets with parchment paper.
2. In a medium-sized saucepan, combine the butter, water, salt, and sugar. Heat over medium heat until the butter is melted and the mixture comes to a boil.
3. Add the flour all at once and stir vigorously with a wooden spoon until the mixture forms a ball and pulls away from the sides of the pan.
4. Remove the pan from the heat and let the mixture cool for 5 minutes.
5. Add the eggs one at a time, stirring vigorously after each addition, until the mixture is smooth and shiny.
6. Transfer the dough to a piping bag fitted with a large round tip. Pipe small mounds of dough onto the prepared baking sheets, about 1 inch in diameter and 1 inch apart.
7. Bake for 20-25 minutes, or until the profiteroles are golden brown and puffed. Remove from the oven and let cool completely on a wire rack.
8. To make the pastry cream, combine the milk and 1/4 cup of the sugar in a medium-sized saucepan. Heat over medium heat until the mixture comes to a simmer.
9. In a separate bowl, whisk together the egg yolks, cornstarch, remaining 1/4 cup sugar, vanilla extract, and salt.
10. Slowly pour the hot milk mixture into the egg mixture, whisking constantly. Return the mixture to the saucepan and cook over medium heat, stirring constantly, until thickened.
11. Remove from heat and let cool to room temperature.
12. In a separate bowl, whip the heavy cream until stiff peaks form.
13. Fold the whipped cream into the cooled pastry cream.
14. Fill a piping bag fitted with a small round tip with the pastry cream. Use the tip to fill each profiterole with the cream.
15. To make the caramel, combine the sugar, water, and corn syrup in a medium-sized saucepan. Heat over medium heat until the sugar has dissolved.
16. Increase the heat to high and cook the mixture, without stirring, until it turns a deep amber color.
17. Remove from heat and let the caramel cool for a minute or two.
18. Dip the tops of the profiteroles into the caramel, then stack them into a cone shape on a serving platter. Use more caramel to attach the profiteroles to each other.
19. Let the caramel harden before serving. Optionally, decorate with additional caramel drizzle or powdered sugar.
